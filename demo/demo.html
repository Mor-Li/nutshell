Spectral Collapse of Low-Rank Adapters
under Non-Stationary Curricula

Anonymous Authors · Under review

Abstract. We study the dynamics of parameter-efficient fine-tuning when the data distribution drifts during training. We show that the singular spectrum of the adapter update ΔW = BA concentrates onto a vanishing number of directions, a phenomenon we term spectral collapse, and that the collapse rate is governed by the curvature mismatch between successive curriculum phases. Under mild assumptions on the Hessian we derive a bound relating the effective rank reff(ΔW) to the drift magnitude, and propose a nuclear-norm regularizer that provably preserves rank. Experiments on 14 instruction-tuning benchmarks confirm the predicted phase transition.

1  Introduction

Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) freezes the pretrained weights W0 and learns a factorized update ΔW = BA with B ∈ ℝd×r, A ∈ ℝr×k and r ≪ min(d, k). While its efficiency is well documented, comparatively little is known about how the learned subspace evolves when the curriculum is non-stationary, as is the norm in continual instruction tuning, preference optimization with refreshed rollouts, and multi-stage domain adaptation. Empirically, practitioners report that adapters trained in this regime plateau early and degrade silently; we argue the two observations share a common spectral cause.

Our analysis tracks the normalized singular values σ̃i = σi / ‖ΔW‖F through training. When consecutive phases induce Hessians with misaligned top eigenspaces, gradient noise projected onto the shared subspace is amplified, whereas orthogonal components are damped at rate proportional to the mismatch κ. The resulting dynamics admit a closed-form fixed point at which all but O(1) directions vanish.

We make three contributions. First, we formalize spectral collapse and show it is distinct from rank deficiency at initialization. Second, we prove that the effective rank decays as exp(−κ t / τ) under a drift model with timescale τ. Third, we introduce a nuclear-norm penalty λ‖ΔW‖* that restores the spread of the spectrum at negligible cost, improving average accuracy by 3.1 points across benchmarks while matching the parameter budget of standard LoRA.
